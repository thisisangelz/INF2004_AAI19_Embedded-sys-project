# TCS34725 colour-mat calibration, 6 November 2026

Measurements of the robot's front colour sensor on the five-ring pink mat,
taken with `tcs34725_calib` (gain setting 1 = 4x, 103 ms integration). The
robot stood on its wheels so the sensor was at its mounted height. Each capture
is 20 readings at one spot with the robot stationary. Three rounds were taken
at different spots.

`C` is overall brightness. `G/R` and `B/R` are colour ratios x1000.

## The "other" surface in each round

| Round | "Other" surface |
|---|---|
| 1 | Shadow of the beacon holder, lying on the mat (ring 5) |
| 2 | Shadow of a person squatting beside the mat, on the floor |
| 3 | Shadow of a chair, on the floor |

In round 1 the robot was moving while the first `LIVE` floor lines printed.
Those live lines are not part of the summary; the captured floor values were
taken stationary (spread 3066–3068).

## Findings

1. **`G/R` identifies the ring and is repeatable.** Across three spots it varied
   by at most 14 per surface, while neighbouring rings differ by about 150.
   Brightness `C` varied by up to 15% between spots, so it is the weaker cue.
2. **Shadows do not change the ring.** The sensor lights the surface with its
   own LED. A shadow lowers `C` slightly but leaves `G/R` almost unchanged: the
   shadow on ring 5 still read as ring 5, and both floor shadows still read as
   floor. A shadow cannot make the floor look like a pink ring.
3. **The floor needs both values.** Its `G/R` (908–931 including shadows)
   overlaps ring 1 (924–931), but its `C` (at most 3068) is far below ring 1
   (at least 6255). Its `C` overlaps ring 4, but its `G/R` is far above ring 4.
   Using `G/R` first and `C` only between floor and ring 1 separates all of them.
4. White edge and ring 1 are closest in `C` (round 2: 6792 against 6477), but
   `G/R` separates them by at least 75.
5. The first version of the tool compared the floor as if it were lighter than
   the mat and reported `OVERLAP` for floor against white edge and ring 1. That
   check was wrong, not the data. Rounds 2 and 3 were run with that version too;
   the fixed tool compares the floor and other surfaces in both directions.

## Proposed classification

Thresholds sit midway between the neighbouring overall ranges:

| `G/R` x1000 | Surface |
|---|---|
| 968 and above | White edge |
| 856–967 | Ring 1 if `C` > 4660, otherwise floor |
| 707–855 | Ring 2 |
| 552–706 | Ring 3 |
| 409–551 | Ring 4 |
| 408 and below | Ring 5 |

Smallest margin from a measured value to a threshold: 37 (white edge against
968) for `G/R`, and about 1590 counts (floor and ring 1 against 4660) for `C`.

Proposed arrival rule for the robot: **ring 4 or darker for three consecutive
readings, after a lighter mat ring (1–3) has been seen during the approach**,
plus a loose BLE check that AP1 is nearby. Ring 4 keeps the robot's front clear
of the beacon box. The lighter-ring requirement guards against strongly red or
pink objects on the floor, which were not measured.

## Update, 7 November: floor read as ring 1 under brighter light

On 7 November every surface read 30-60% brighter than in this calibration.
In the [side-on run](../runs/2026-11-07-side-on-mat-ring1-transfer.md) four
floor stops had `C` 4686-5012 with floor `G/R` (958-963), so the `C` > 4660 rule
called them ring 1. `G/R` alone cannot fix this: the floor's `G/R` reached 965,
between ring 1 and the white edge.

`B/R` (blue/red x1000), which like `G/R` does not change with brightness,
separates them in all 7 November logs:

| Surface | `B/R` x1000 |
|---|---|
| Floor (53 readings, including the four misread stops) | 724-813 |
| Ring 1 | 879-950 |
| White edge, including dim readings at the edge | 871-986 |
| Ring 2 | 733-838 (already separated by `G/R`) |

The classifier now also requires `B/R` >= 842 (the midpoint) for white edge
and ring 1; below it the reading is floor. Rings 2-5 are unchanged. This
calibration did not record blue values, so its ring 1 and white-edge `B/R` is
not known; a quick recapture with the calibration tool would confirm the
margin under that lighting.
